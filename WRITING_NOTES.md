# Writing notes — Ian's voice, sharpened

> A working reference for editing Ian's articles before publishing.
> **Read this before proofing any new post.** After the typo pass, run every
> new draft against the checklist at the bottom and flag anything that misses.

---

## What's already working — do NOT flatten these

These are the load-bearing traits of the voice. If an edit weakens any of them,
the edit is wrong.

### 1. Present-tense narration with retrospective interjection
Ian slips between "I am packing my fanny pack" (in-the-moment) and "Years later,
I look at the footage" (reflection) without warning. That's not sloppy — it's
how memory actually works. The vivid parts are present; the interpretation is
past. Keep this rhythm.

### 2. Specificity is the superpower
- 2023 S-Class. 400 dirhams. 25 degrees. The Filipino dude at the counter.
- The Radisson (named), not "a nice hotel".
- The pyramid-shaped building. The wetsuit smelling of fish.

Never smooth these out. Vague writers describe "a nice hotel"; Ian names the
brand. If an edit removes a proper noun, a number, or a physical detail —
**revert it**.

### 3. Sentence rhythm — short punches after long builds
Signature moves:
- "A camel."
- "Hell yeah I do!"
- "Yet."
- "Period." after a complex clause.

If a draft has three long paragraphs in a row with no punch, note it.

### 4. He resists moralizing
The Dubai piece ends with "That pride has carried me through hundreds of dives"
— one line. He doesn't hammer it. He trusts the reader.

**Red flag**: any closing that reads like a call to action, a "lesson learned",
or a takeaway list. That's not the voice. Kill it.

### 5. Dark-humored self-deprecation, not motivational fluff
"My posture is awkward, and I look a little aloof… Yet, I look like a diver."
The self-deprecation earns the confidence. Never let this drift into either
false modesty or "I overcame my fears and you can too" energy.

### 6. Full forms over contractions (blog voice)
"I do not believe him" — not "I don't". This gives the prose a deliberate,
slightly formal weight against the casual content. It's a choice, and it works.
**Exception**: actual spoken dialogue can use contractions to sound real.

### 7. Em dashes for asides and pauses
"I am a diver — a newbie — but I am crushing this today."
"Even if you did — you are still deep enough for water to be over your head."
Use em dashes (—), not hyphens (-), not double hyphens (--).

## Where to push him (edit gently)

### 1. Tense collapse on reflection lines
Occasional stumbles like "That gave me a sense of comfort" mid-paragraph while
everything around it is present-tense. Not wrong — just a stumble a reader
notices.

**Fix**: either commit to present ("That gives me…") *or* set the reflective
line off as its own paragraph in past tense. Don't leave it as a wobble
inside a present-tense paragraph.

### 2. Comma splices — leave the stylistic ones, cut the ambiguous ones
Ian writes like he talks. Extra commas ("The ride was smooth, and uneventful
in the best way") give the prose a breathy, thinking-out-loud quality. **Do
not** clean these up mechanically.

Only cut a comma splice if it creates real ambiguity or genuinely stops the
reader.

### 3. Openings could be knife-sharper
He's capable of harder first sentences than he sometimes writes. Look at the
first paragraph and ask: **could this open with the concrete object instead
of the scene-setting clause?**

- Softer: "On a sweltering July night in Ras Al Khaimah, I am packing my fanny pack…"
- Sharper: "It is a sweltering July night in Ras Al Khaimah, and I am packing cash into a fanny pack."

The first version is fine. The second drops the reader in one beat faster.
Flag openings that could benefit — but never rewrite unless he asks.

### 4. Trust the reader more on setups
Rhetorical questions like *"Have you ever been in a situation so stressful…?"*
are slightly weaker than just **stating** the paradox and letting the reader
nod. If a paragraph asks the reader a question that could be a statement,
flag it.

### 5. Contractions in dialogue
When someone is literally speaking out loud, "I don't know" sounds more real
than "I do not know". This is the one place to relax the no-contractions rule.

## Structural instincts he already has

- **He'll spend 40% of a dive story getting to the dive.** That is a choice,
  not a mistake. The mundane details (Mercedes, mall, bandana, camel) build
  the texture of his life at the time so the payoff has a fully-realized
  world around it. Some pieces need this slow build; others need to open in
  the water. Don't force one shape onto every post.
- **The shape is the product.** Editors, AI, and SEO tools will all want to
  shorten sentences and flatten paragraphs. Resist all of it.

## Pre-publish checklist (run this on every draft)

For every new article, check each item and **report which ones pass/fail
before publishing**. Do not silently "fix" — flag first, let Ian decide.

### Voice
- [ ] Present-tense narration is consistent within each paragraph (no wobbly reflection lines mid-paragraph)
- [ ] At least one short-punch sentence per major beat ("A camel." energy)
- [ ] No motivational-speaker closing / call to action / takeaway list
- [ ] No corporate language, no "we" when he means "I"
- [ ] No contractions in editorial prose (dialogue exception is fine)
- [ ] Em dashes are actual em dashes (—), not hyphens
- [ ] Specific proper nouns and numbers preserved (no "a nice hotel", no "some drinks")

### Openings
- [ ] First sentence puts the reader in a scene or on a concrete object — not a rhetorical question, not a mission statement
- [ ] Flag if the opening could be sharper by starting with the noun instead of the scene-setting clause

### Trust the reader
- [ ] No rhetorical question where a statement would do the same work
- [ ] No over-explaining a technical term he'd use naturally (pp02, deco, narcosis, reg, twin set)
- [ ] Closing lands in one line, not a paragraph

### Housekeeping (from AUTHORING.md)
- [ ] Frontmatter is raw YAML — **not wrapped in a code block**
- [ ] `excerpt` is real (140–160 chars), not the template placeholder
- [ ] `cover` path resolves and image is in `public/posts/<slug>/`
- [ ] All inline images have alt text
- [ ] Every image referenced actually exists on disk
- [ ] Typos, dropped words ("I among" → "I am among"), and misspellings caught

### What to tell Ian before pub
Format the report as:

```
✅ Passes: voice, openings, closing, images
⚠️  Flags:
    - Paragraph 6: tense wobble ("That gave me..." mid present-tense block)
    - Opening: could sharpen by dropping "On a sweltering..." → "It is a sweltering..."
    - Paragraph 9: rhetorical question could be a statement
🔧 Auto-fixed: 3 typos, 1 missing "am", cover path
```

Then ask: **"Publish as-is, apply the flagged changes, or hold?"**

---

## Volume matters

The voice is set. What sharpens it now is reps. If a draft is 85% there,
publish it — a slightly imperfect real post beats a perfect unwritten one.
Only hold if a structural problem (wrong tense throughout, closing that
betrays the voice, obvious factual error) would embarrass him.
